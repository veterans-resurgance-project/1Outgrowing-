AFTI Portal: Strategic Partnership & Participant Roadmap

[cite: 1] This document outlines the operational roadmap, mutual growth framework, and step-by-step strategy for engaging agricultural partners, senior homesteaders, and AFTI participants across the Willamette Valley.

1. The Mutual Growth Framework

[cite: 1] Our vocational program and regional farm network operate as a single, supportive ecosystem. What our trainees learn in the classroom is shaped directly by what our partners need in the field.

What AFTI Brings to the Land

[cite: 1] Reliable, energetic, and disciplined teams trained in core safety, task execution, and crop care. For smaller homesteads and senior growers, we also bridge the digital divide—assisting with direct-to-consumer tech, record-keeping, and translating extension service guidance into active field help.

What Farms Bring Back to AFTI

[cite: 1] Authentic living classrooms where our participants gain real-world experience. Experienced growers and legacy farmers pass down invaluable generational wisdom, crop intuition, and regional resilience strategies to our cohorts.

2. Step-by-Step Strategic Roadmap

Phase 1: Extension Partnership & Target Identification

  • [cite: 1] Engage local Oregon State University (OSU) Extension agents at inception as co-creators, positioning AFTI trainee crews as a workforce multiplier to execute recommendations on neglected small-scale or senior-owned properties.
  • [cite: 1] Identify target homesteads (e.g., senior operators or solo widows managing 5–40 acres) where physical fatigue or administrative gridlock prevents optimal yield and maintenance.

Phase 2: Flagship Team Micro-Farm Pilot

  • [cite: 1] Establish the first flagship team micro-farm on a partnered senior homestead with intensive extension support.
  • [cite: 1] Deploy disciplined trainee crews to provide immediate manual relief (clearing brush, pruning fruit trees, repairing irrigation) and rescue crops from going to waste.

Phase 3: Graduate Progression & Shared Harvest Expansion

  • [cite: 1] Participants who successfully complete a full planting and harvest season earn advancement incentives, such as advanced agricultural leadership scholarships or priority access to independent micro-plot incubators.
  • [cite: 1] Spin out future micro-farm situations as graduates step into leadership roles, serving as visible models for new recruits.

3. Partnership Request & Intake Framework

[cite: 1] To streamline onboarding, we collect structured requests from agricultural partners and advisors across three core tiers:

  1. [cite: 1] Senior Homesteaders & Legacy Operators: Manual relief, digital assistance, and orchard/crop recovery support.
  2. [cite: 1] Mid-Sized Diversified Farms: Seasonal labor surge support aligned with specific standard operating procedures (SOPs).
  3. [cite: 1] Large Enterprises: Dedicated pipeline pre-screened for safety compliance and equipment handling.
