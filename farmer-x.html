Chapter One: The Homestead

The game opens on the porch of Farmer X’s homestead just outside Lebanon, Oregon. A rusted John Deere sits idle in the distance, its front loader frozen mid-lift. A weathered foreclosure notice from the bank is pinned to the screen door, fluttering in the early autumn wind.

Farmer X sits nursing a cracked rib and a heavily braced arm, staring at empty ledger books. For twenty years, he did everything alone until a PTO shaft caught his jacket. Now, a lost season has wiped out cash flow, and his traditional wholesale buyer dropped his contract.

As the incoming trainee, you must step up and open the first ledger to learn how the business actually survives.

Chapter 1 (Quest 1): The Crop Diversification Trap

The Dilemma: Farmer X relied 100% on a single commodity crop that just crashed in market value.

Game Challenge: Select the strategy that protects a small farm from total market collapse.

Survival Sidetrack: The Foraging Loop

Setback: Chasing single-commodity booms leaves you broke when the market shifts. Farmer X sends you out to the timber lot to learn resilience.

Lesson: You forage for wild chanterelles and oyster mushrooms under an off-grid botanist, learning natural diversity before returning to business realities.

Chapter Two: The Tool Shed & Soil Management

The rusted hinges of the tool shed groan open, releasing the sharp scent of linseed oil and iron. Farmer X tosses you a tarnished brass soil core sampler.

"The ledger numbers kept the bank from locking the front gate today," Farmer X grunts. "Now comes the real test. If your dirt is dead, everything you plant turns to dust before July."

Chapter 2 (Quest 1): The Acidic Lower Bottomland

The Dilemma: Your soil meter fluctuates before settling on an acidic 5.4 pH in the lower bottomland plots, locking up essential phosphorus.

Game Challenge: Determine the correct corrective action.

Consulting the Extension Agent

Extension Advisor: "Chemical quick-fixes on severe acid soils just burn out your microbial life. Review your lime requirement calculations and buffer curves."
